This supernatural promise awakens a desperate ambition. In a soliloquy, Macbeth confesses he has “no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only / Vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself / And falls on the other” (1.7.25-28). Lady Macbeth, initially ruthless, later sleepwalks, trying to scrub away the psychological stain: “Out, damned spot! out, I say!” (5.1.37). The imaginary blood betrays a soul shattered by guilt.
