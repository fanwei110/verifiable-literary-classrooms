Hospitality, or xenia, functions as both a moral code and a tool for discerning friend from foe. This contrast emphasizes that true belonging comes not just from bloodline, but from adherence to social and ethical norms. The suitors' abuse of hospitality ultimately justifies their downfall, as they disrupt the very foundation of Ithacan society. As the narrator states, 'No use, Cyclops, to ask their pity— / they have no law, they care for no right / of others,' illustrating the danger of lawless behavior. Conversely, Odysseus asserts, 'I am Odysseus, son of Laertes... known to the world for wisdom,' reclaiming his identity and rightful place.
