Adam and Eve are not overcome by the fruit’s allure alone; they choose to eat, exercising the free will that makes their fall both possible and morally significant. This human rebellion echoes the earlier, cosmic rebellion of Satan. Refusing to serve, Satan famously declares, “Better to reign in Hell, then serve in Heav’n.” The line crystallizes how pride twists free will into defiant self-assertion. Satan’s choice to rule in misery rather than obey in bliss reveals that temptation operates not by forcing the hand but by seducing the will to mistake servitude for slavery and rebellion for freedom. Milton thus presents temptation as the occasion, not the cause, of sin: true catastrophe springs from a free creature’s inward decision to rebel.
