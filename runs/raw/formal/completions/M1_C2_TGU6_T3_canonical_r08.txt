Identity is fluid yet essential; Odysseus’s name and reputation precede him, but he must prove himself anew, as seen when he strings his bow to win Penelope’s hand. Hospitality, governed by *xenia*, is a sacred duty that exposes the moral decay of Ithaca. The suitors’ abuse of Odysseus’s home violates this code, justifying their violent downfall. These themes intertwine: hospitality preserves social order, identity is affirmed through recognition, and homecoming restores balance.
