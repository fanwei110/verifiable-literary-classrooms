  "answer": "In Homer's Odyssey, the themes of homecoming (nostos), identity, and hospitality (xenia) are tightly interwoven. The epic opens by announcing Odysseus's deep longing to return to Ithaca: \"he, though he was longing to return to his wife and country, was detained by the goddess Calypso\" (Book I). This desire for home defines his identity; he is not a wanderer by choice but a husband and king yearning for his proper place. Yet homecoming is not merely a physical journey. When Odysseus, shipwrecked and naked, meets Nausicaa, he invokes the ideal of a shared home as a blessing: \"May heaven grant you in all things your heart’s desire—husband, house, and a happy, peaceful home; for there is nothing better in this world than that man and wife should be of one mind in a house.\" Here, home is presented as the supreme good, a partnership that defeats enemies and gladdens friends. His speech is also an act of hospitality-seeking, praising the very institution that the Phaeacians, through their generous welcome, will help him reclaim.
