Odysseus’ long journey home is not just a physical return but a restoration of his identity as king, husband, and father. Conversely, the suitors’ abuse of hospitality in Odysseus’ home underscores their moral corruption and foreshadows their downfall. Identity is also fluid in the epic; Odysseus often conceals his true self, revealing it only when the moment demands. His homecoming is incomplete until he is recognized by those who matter most, proving that identity is both personal and relational.
