  "answer": "In Homer’s *Odyssey*, nostos (homecoming) is never just a physical return; it is a test of identity and a negotiation of xenia (hospitality). Odysseus must repeatedly decide when to reveal or conceal who he is, and the welcome he receives—or fails to receive—shapes his journey. For instance, trapped in the Cyclops’ cave, Odysseus tells Polyphemus, “Nobody — that’s my name. Nobody — so my mother and father call me, all my friends.” This cunning false identity allows escape, but it also shows how the violation of xenia—the host eating guests—forces the hero to suppress his true self to survive.
