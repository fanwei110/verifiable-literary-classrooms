Hospitality (*xenia*), a sacred Greek custom, structures interactions between hosts and guests, often revealing moral character. The suitors’ abuse of hospitality in Odysseus’s home underscores their corruption, while figures like Eumaeus the swineherd embody its ideals. These themes intertwine: hospitality can either aid or obstruct homecoming, and identity is often confirmed through how one is received—or rejected—by others.
