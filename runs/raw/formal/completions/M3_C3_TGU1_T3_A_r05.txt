This internal struggle is further complicated by the theme of performance. Characters constantly assume roles: Claudius plays the noble king while harboring guilt, Polonius uses Ophelia as a pawn in a scheme of surveillance, and Hamlet himself adopts an 'antic disposition' to mask his true intentions. These performances blur truth and illusion, reinforcing Hamlet’s inability to trust or act with certainty. The play thus presents action not as a straightforward response to injustice, but as a complex negotiation between inner turmoil, fear of death, and the masks people wear. As Hamlet reflects, the prospect of death is terrifying precisely because 'in that sleep of death what dreams may come'—an unknown that halts resolution.
